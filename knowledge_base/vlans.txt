VLAN Study Notes

VLAN stands for Virtual Local Area Network.

A VLAN logically separates devices into different broadcast domains on a switch.

Devices in different VLANs normally cannot communicate directly at Layer 2. Communication between VLANs requires a Layer 3 device such as a router or multilayer switch.

An access port normally carries traffic for a single VLAN.

A trunk port can carry traffic for multiple VLANs between network devices.

802.1Q is a common VLAN tagging standard used on trunk links.

For example, VLAN 10 could be used for students and VLAN 20 could be used for staff.

VLANs can improve network organization, segmentation, and security.

Inter-VLAN routing allows devices in different VLANs to communicate through a Layer 3 device.
